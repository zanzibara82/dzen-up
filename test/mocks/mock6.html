2026 Subaru Outback Wilderness: long-form review

Part 1.1 — The 2026 Subaru Outback Wilderness arrives as the most off-road-focused version of the Outback lineup, and it keeps the rugged styling cues that made the previous generation so recognizable on the trail.

Part 1.2 — Ground clearance measures 9.5 inches, which is roughly half an inch more than the standard Outback, and the suspension has been retuned with longer travel to soak up rough terrain at speed.

Part 1.3 — Standard all-terrain tires wrap around 17-inch wheels, and the front and rear bumpers have been reshaped to improve approach and departure angles on steep obstacles.

Part 1.4 — Power comes from a turbocharged 2.4-liter boxer four-cylinder engine that produces 260 horsepower and 277 pound-feet of torque, paired exclusively with a continuously variable transmission.

Part 1.5 — Towing capacity is rated at 3,500 pounds, which matches the standard Outback with the same engine, and the roof rails are rated for a static load of 700 pounds.

Part 1.6 — Inside, the cabin uses water-repellent upholstery and all-weather floor mats, and the cargo area is trimmed with a durable plastic liner that is easy to wipe down after a muddy weekend.

Part 1.7 — The infotainment system runs on an 11.6-inch portrait-oriented touchscreen, and wireless smartphone mirroring is standard on every trim level.

Part 1.8 — Fuel economy is estimated at 26 miles per gallon on the highway and 21 in the city, which is slightly lower than the standard Outback because of the heavier tires and the additional body cladding.

Part 1.9 — Safety equipment includes adaptive cruise control, lane centering, automatic emergency braking, and a blind-spot monitoring system with rear cross-traffic alert.

Part 1.10 — Pricing starts at roughly 40,000 dollars before destination charges, and the Wilderness trim sits near the top of the Outback range.

Part 1.11 — Competitors include the Toyota RAV4 TRD Off-Road, the Ford Bronco Sport Badlands, and the Mazda CX-50 Meridian Edition, all of which target the same adventurous buyer.

Part 1.12 — The verdict: the Outback Wilderness is not a rock crawler, but it is a genuinely capable all-weather wagon that handles gravel roads, snow, and muddy trails with far more confidence than a standard family hauler.
